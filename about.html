About Mayo ASA

A community built on culture, food, and showing up for each other.

Here's what Mayo ASA is really about, and where the idea came from.

Mission

Our mission

We want Mayo ASA to be a real community for Asian American, Pacific Islander, and Desi students — and allies — built around culture and connection, with room to actually help people too.

Many students are searching for a real sense of belonging and a way to connect over shared roots, especially since our school lacks any Asian language or cultural classes. While our school already has a vibrant network of culture clubs, an ASA fills the missing gap—giving Asian students and allies a dedicated space to celebrate identity while serving as an ambitious engine to tackle real-world issues, like raising funds for disaster relief overseas.

Wilson Wang, Founder
What we're about

Culture and community, first

We — Wilson Wang, Chen Liang, Kevin Wang, and Nathaniel Yuan — started Mayo ASA because we wanted Asian, Asian American, Pacific Islander, and Desi students at Mayo to have a real space of their own. Regular meetings, cultural celebrations, food, traditions — honestly, just a community that didn't already exist here.

You don't have to be Asian, Pacific Islander, or Desi to join, either — allies are just as welcome, no questions asked. And when we've got the energy for it, we try to turn that community into something bigger, like raising money for Nepal flood relief.

Beyond Mayo

Giving back doesn't stop at Mayo

We're running a fundraiser for Nepal flood relief, to help communities recovering from the flash floods that hit Nepal in August 2026 — real relief, not just a hashtag.

It's not some separate side project — it's just what happens when people who already look out for each other decide to look a little further. Check out our Global Impact page for the full details and how to help.
